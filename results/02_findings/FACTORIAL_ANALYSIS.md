## 5. The best cell on pooled PCC is NOT the best cell on a_p

| criterion | winning cell | pooled PCC | a_p | r | s | oracle | gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| best pooled ddG PCC | `a1_d0_s1_D0_coil` | ** 0.6100** |  0.5825 |  0.7527 |  0.8070 |  0.7281 |  0.1181 |
| best a_p | `a1_d1_s1_D0_coil` |  0.5874 | ** 0.7098** |  0.7429 |  0.9860 |  0.7092 |  0.1219 |

**They are DIFFERENT cells.** `a1_d0_s1_D0_coil` wins pooled PCC (0.6100, with a_p 0.5825); `a1_d1_s1_D0_coil` wins
a_p (0.7098, with pooled PCC 0.5874). Selecting on pooled PCC therefore picks the
cell with the WORSE slope, and selecting on a_p picks the cell with the worse
pooled correlation.

That contrast is the calibration thesis in one line: **pooled PCC is dominated by
the between-protein offset b_p, while a_p measures the within-protein compression.**
A model can rank proteins well against each other and still be flat inside every
one of them. The two metrics answer different questions, and the model-selection
metric has to be chosen deliberately rather than inherited.

## 6. Is the D1 half worth finishing?

Scored so far: **6 D1 cells** against **6 D0 cells**.

- D1 pooled PCC: -0.0366 .. 0.3056
- D0 pooled PCC: 0.5808 .. 0.6100
- D1 a_p: 0.0044 .. 0.0220 -- an a_p near zero means the model is FLAT in ddG and
  barely responds to mutation at all.

The statistical argument, in the form that actually decides it:

1. **Separation, not a mean difference.** Every D1 cell is below every D0 cell
   (6 vs 6, no overlap). Under the null that D has no effect, the chance that a
   random labelling puts all 6 D1 runs below all 6 D0 runs is 1 / C(12,6) = 0.001082.
   That is an exact permutation p-value and it needs no distributional assumption.

2. **The effect is enormous relative to the only noise estimate we have.** The five
   seed replicates give sd = 0.0339 on pooled PCC; the D gap is 0.4359, i.e. 13 sd.
   Remaining D1 cells would have to be many sd ABOVE every D1 cell seen so far to
   change the conclusion.

3. **What more D1 cells could buy.** They cannot overturn the sign; they could only
   sharpen the estimate of a quantity we would not use. The purpose of a factorial is
   to estimate A, B, C main effects and their interactions -- but a_p in D1 is
   0.0044..0.0220, so the model there is near-flat and A/B/C are being measured on top of
   a dead signal. An interaction estimated inside a collapsed arm is not informative
   about the arm we ship.

4. **The mechanism is already understood, which is what makes the negative safe to
   act on.** D1 zeroes the unfolded embedding; that removes the offset by removing
   the SIGNAL, which is the same failure already on record for `noemb` on dG
   (lowest MAE, correlation 0.35 -> 0.05). A negative with a known mechanism does not
   need the same replication as an unexplained one.

5. **A nuance that must not be glossed, because it is the one thing D1 does show.**
   D1 pooled PCC collapses to -0.0366..0.3056, but the D1 ORACLE stays at 0.3428..0.3912, and
   the D1 oracle GAIN (0.0793..0.3793) is LARGER than any D0 gain. Read that carefully:
   removing the unfolded embedding did NOT destroy all ranking information -- it
   destroyed the SLOPE (a_p 0.0044..0.0220, i.e. flat) and inflated the between-protein
   offset, so an oracle that is *allowed to fit each protein offset from the test
   labels* recovers a good deal. This is the metric rule biting again: the oracle is
   an upper bound that rewards exactly the failure D1 introduces, so **the D1 oracle
   must not be read as partial success.** The shippable quantity is a_p, and a_p is
   dead. It also means the honest summary of D1 is "collapses the slope", not
   "collapses the model".

**Conclusion: finishing the remaining D1 cells is not justified.** The honest
statement of the limitation is that the D1 arm is scored at n=6 rather than n=24,
and it should be written that way -- as a stopped arm with an exact permutation
p-value and a mechanism, not as a completed one.

The counter-argument that deserves recording: a 2^4 factorial with a collapsed arm
cannot estimate A x D, B x D or C x D interactions, so the write-up must claim main
effects **conditional on D0** and not a full factorial decomposition.

## 7. What this analysis does NOT establish

- Every factorial cell is a **single seed**. Nothing here is replicated at the cell level;
  the noise floor is imported from a different configuration (`abl_sigma`) and assumed to
  transfer. That assumption is doing real work and should be stated in the thesis.
- The D0 arm is **not yet balanced**: with 6 of 24 cells the A/B/C contrasts are
  partially confounded with which cells happened to finish first. Counts are printed above.
- Cells are read at DIFFERENT epochs (the `ep` column), selected per run. Epoch selection
  is itself a lever, so part of any cell-to-cell difference is a checkpoint-choice
  difference rather than a factor effect. Section 4.0 measures this directly, and on the
  cells scored so far it is not a hypothetical: at least one factor is fully aliased with
  seed and epoch, and its apparently large effect is therefore NOT interpretable.
- The seed replicates (`abl_sigma`) fix only the seed. They do NOT bound epoch-selection
  variance, so the "2-run threshold" is a LOWER bound on the true run-to-run spread and
  every "exceeds the noise band" verdict is correspondingly optimistic.
- n=28 proteins. Per FINDINGS, |r| < 0.374 is indistinguishable from zero at this n for
  any protein-level correlation quoted here.
