## How to read this

Every number carries three qualifiers: *pooled-or-per-protein* / *dG-or-ddG* / *which
split*. All rows here are the **test** split on the **27-protein** basis.

| column | definition | side |
|---|---|---|
| `pooled` | PCC over every row of every protein pooled together | ddG |
| `oracle` | subtract each protein's own mean residual, then pool. **Uses the test labels -- an upper bound, not a method.** | ddG |
| `gain` | `oracle - pooled`: what solving b_p would be worth on this run | ddG |
| `perprot` | **median** per-protein PCC -- ranking quality *within* a protein | ddG |
| `a_p` | median per-protein slope, `a_p = r * s` | ddG |
| `r` | median per-protein PCC (the ranking half of a_p) | ddG |
| `s` | median `std(pred)/std(true)` (the spread half of a_p) | ddG |
| `std(b_p)` | sd over proteins of the WT error `pred_dG - dG` on row 0 | **dG** |

**The metric rule (MASTER 2.1).** ddG cancels anything identical between wild type and
mutant, so a reference-state or whole-protein lever must be read on **dG or b_p**.
`a_p` is a *within*-protein slope and does not cancel, so it **is** ddG-measurable.
`std(b_p)` and `dG_MAE` are the dG-side columns; they are never averaged into the ddG ones.

**The 2K5H basis matters.** `scripts/factorial_analysis.py` *shifts* 2K5H by -3.0824 and
keeps 28 proteins; MASTER.md *drops* it and keeps 27. The two differ:
`sigma_seed2_e10` scores 0.6382 dropped, 0.6386 shifted, 0.6575 uncorrected. MASTER 1.3
quotes **0.6382**, so **drop** is canonical and is what this table uses.

**The seed noise band is +/-0.060 pooled** (five `abl_sigma` seeds, identical config).
Every group below is reported as mean +/- sd so it can be read against that band.
**A difference under +/-0.060 is not distinguishable from seed noise.**

**Epoch discipline (OPEN_PROBLEMS P0).** Epoch selection is on VALIDATION
(`run_calib_eval.sh` line 15); the test set is scored once at that epoch. Two runs were
deliberately scored at six epochs each to measure a trajectory -- `gld_slope1.0_s42` and
`gld_dg_coil_s42`. **Quoting the best of those six would be test-set peeking**, so this
table keeps only their canonical (val-selected) epoch e14; the other epochs are listed
separately at the bottom and must never be read as run results.

## 3. Best on pooled vs best on a_p -- the calibration thesis in one line

| criterion | winning run | pooled | a_p | r | s | oracle | std(b_p) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **best pooled** | `sigma_seed2` (e10) | **0.6382** | 0.4099 | 0.7906 | 0.5976 | 0.7534 | 1.2512 |
| **best a_p** | `p3_a1_d1_s1_D0_coil_seed42` (e13) | 0.5803 | **0.7156** | 0.7332 | 1.0161 | 0.7006 | 1.8663 |
| control | `calib_ctrl_repro2` (e14) | 0.5635 | 0.4960 | 0.7929 | 0.6351 | 0.7364 | 1.5752 |

**They are DIFFERENT runs -- and that contrast IS the calibration thesis.**

`sigma_seed2` wins pooled at **0.6382** while carrying a_p = 0.4099.  
`p3_a1_d1_s1_D0_coil_seed42` wins a_p at **0.7156** while its pooled is only 0.5803 (+0.0168 vs control).

The two criteria disagree because they measure **two different failures**:

- **`a_p` is a WITHIN-protein slope.** Fixing it is pure calibration: `a_p = r * s`,
  and `--slope_weight` drives `s` toward 1 with `r` flat (MASTER 3.2). It cannot move
  the pooled number much, because pooling is dominated by the *between*-protein offset.
- **`pooled` is dominated by b_p**, the per-protein offset. That is why the offset
  oracle sits **0.1152** above the best pooled run, and why b_p -- not the slope -- is the
  binding constraint on the headline number.

So the run that best *calibrates the slope* is not the run that best *ranks across
proteins*. **Two independent failures, two different winners, one shared ceiling.**

### The slope factor C, inside D0 -- a_p = r * s, on independent cells

| C (`--slope_weight`) | n | pooled | a_p | **r** | **s** | std(b_p) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off (s0) | 4 | 0.5995 | 0.4658 | 0.7585 | 0.6372 | 1.4460 |
| on (s1) | 3 | 0.5865 | 0.5527 | 0.7584 | 0.7870 | 1.5597 |
| **effect** | | **-0.0131** | **+0.0869** | **-0.0002** | **+0.1497** | **+0.1137** |

**This is the `a_p = r * s` identity reproducing itself on cells that were never used
to derive it.** Turning the slope lever on moves `a_p` by +0.0869 and `s` by +0.1497 while
`r` stays flat at -0.0002 -- inside the noise, exactly as the identity demands: the lever
cannot manufacture ranking, only spread.

And it shows the **cost** in the same table. Pooled moves -0.0131 (nothing, against a
+/-0.060 band) while `std(b_p)` **worsens by +0.1137**. The arm buys within-protein
calibration and pays for it in between-protein offset. That trade is the reason the
pooled winner and the a_p winner are different runs.
